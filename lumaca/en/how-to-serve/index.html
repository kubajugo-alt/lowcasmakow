How to serve snails

Each jar is a ready dish: 100 g of tender snail meat that takes on the flavour of its sauce. Three ways, each in 5 minutes.

  1. 1

    On toast

    Snails in lemon and parsley oil, warmed for 2–3 minutes, on toasted baguette rubbed with garlic. Parsley and a squeeze of lemon.

  2. 2

    With pasta

    Snails in green pesto or tomato sauce, sautéed for 3–4 minutes and tossed with tagliatelle or spaghetti. A spoonful of pasta water makes it silky.

  3. 3

    In a tartlet

    Ready-made shortcrust tartlets filled with snails in court-bouillon, garlic butter and grated cheese. 5–7 minutes at 200°C.

Snails, demystified

What exactly is it?

A ready dish: 100 g of tender snail meat in stock, oil or sauce, 250 g per jar. The meat is already cooked and seasoned; just heat it.

What do snails taste like?

Delicate, a little like mushrooms or mussels. The texture is tender and springy, and the meat takes on the flavour of its sauce: garlic, lemon, pesto.

Are there shells in the jar?

No. Our jars contain snail meat only, ready to eat once warmed.

Where do the snails come from?

From Polish snail farms. We cook them in our small, veterinary-approved kitchen in Kołomań, in the Świętokrzyskie region.

How should I store it?

Unopened jars keep for 12 months at room temperature in a cool, dry place. Once opened, refrigerate and eat within 3 days.

Allergens?

Snails are molluscs, one of the allergens listed on food labels. The pesto contains walnuts and cheese, the tomato sauce contains celery. Full ingredients are listed for each jar.
