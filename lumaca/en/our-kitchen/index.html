Our kitchen

A forgotten Polish delicacy returns to the table. Where it comes from, who cooks it and why it is worth a try.

  1. 1

    Snails were a Polish delicacy

    Our ancestors ate them. Snail recipes already appear in the first Polish cookbook, Stanisław Czerniecki’s “Compendium ferculorum” of 1682.

  2. 2

    Poland forgot them, yet still produces them

    For years Poland has farmed snails and exported them, mainly to France, while hardly eating them at home.

  3. 3

    Lumaca brings them back to the Polish table

    In a modern form: ready-made dishes from Polish-farmed snails, prepared in a small kitchen in Świętokrzyskie. Just heat and serve.

The owners

Grażyna and Tomasz Nawrocki

Lumaca is run by Grażyna Nawrocka and her husband, Tomasz Nawrocki. They started out farming snails and later turned to cooking them: today Lumaca buys snails from trusted Polish farms, and their own recipes are cooked in the kitchen in Kołomań.

Behind every jar

  • Polish farms

    Gros gris snails (Helix aspersa maxima) from Polish farms, with certificates of origin and veterinary certificates.

  • A small kitchen

    Our processing kitchen in Kołomań is veterinary-approved (no. PL26040901). We cook in small batches.

  • Our own recipes

    The snail meat is simmered for 120 minutes in vegetable stock with dry white wine, then finished in sauces made to our recipes.

  • Tested labels and shelf life

    Labels checked by the Polish food quality inspectorate (IJHARS). Twelve months at room temperature, confirmed by SGS laboratory tests.

  • A vintage

    We buy snails once a year, in autumn. Each batch is a natural vintage, as with wine: the jars now hold the 2026 vintage.
